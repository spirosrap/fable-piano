Hum along puts a simulated voice beside the piano: it picks out the tune and hums it, a few seconds of singing prepared ahead of the music.
With “Hold silently” lit, pressing a key lifts its damper without sounding it. Switch it off and play another key: the held strings ring in sympathy.
Every sound is computed. There are no samples. Each string is a bidirectional waveguide; the hammer is a mass with nonlinear felt (F = K·xp) coupled to the string, and the collision is solved at every sample. The harder the strike, the shorter the contact and the richer the overtones. Each key has one to three strings tuned slightly apart and coupled through the bridge, which gives the beating and the two-stage decay. All the strings share one bridge, so strings whose dampers are up resonate with the overtones of the others.
